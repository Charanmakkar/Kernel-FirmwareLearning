# Thirty day revision calendar

This is revision for a learner who already passes the C and basic Linux practical gates. It does not compress the full 52-week program into one month. Choose a primary role using an actual JD when available.

Follow [Thirty_Day_Revision.csv](Thirty_Day_Revision.csv). Normal days take 90 minutes: 15 minutes of closed-book retrieval, 45 minutes of coding or investigation, 20 minutes of verification and 10 minutes updating the error log. From a question range, select two relevant oral questions or one coding task; the range is a selection pool, not a demand to memorize every entry that day.

Days 7, 14, 21 and 28 are 120-minute mock blocks: 60 minutes interviewed, 30 minutes evidence-based review and 30 minutes of targeted rework. Each seven-day block therefore takes 11 hours. Days 29 and 30 add three hours, giving **47 hours across 30 days**. The mocks replace the ordinary daily session.

| Mock | Interview focus | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| Day 7 | Shared C, Linux I/O and one failure | Code, tests and two corrected explanations |
| Day 14 | Primary-track technical depth | Context/lifetime or firmware timing defense with explicit unknowns |
| Day 21 | System design and project defense | Architecture, failure table and acceptance criteria |
| Day 28 | Hiring simulation with a changed requirement | Independent implementation/debugging evidence and a readiness decision |

Keep answers closed during independent work. For an assisted session, record assistance explicitly and perform a later independent retest. When a critical defect remains, replace the next discretionary topic with remediation; do not conceal it behind calendar completion. If an interview is nearer, prioritize the actual role and one defensible project, then state the gaps left open.
